# Experience Evidence Standard

Status: active internal publishing control  
Applies to: `/experience/`, case notes, testimonials, engagement records, service proof and future outcome claims

## Purpose

Prevent founder experience, confidential enquiries and Gurjas engagements from being blended into a single promotional history. Every public record must state whose work it was, when it occurred, what evidence supports it and what cannot safely be disclosed.

## Attribution classes

### V — Publicly verifiable professional experience

Use only when a dated CV, institutional record, publication, professional profile, contract, award record or equivalent source supports the statement.

Required fields:

- named person or organisation to whom the experience belongs;
- date or period;
- exact role;
- bounded activity or output;
- source location and review date;
- limitations on what the record establishes.

### F — Founder-attested confidential pattern

Use for repeated professional enquiries or observations that cannot be published as individual client records.

Required controls:

- describe the recurring pattern, not an invented composite client;
- state that identities are withheld;
- state that the accounts were not collected as a formal dataset where applicable;
- do not publish a numeric prevalence estimate without a contemporaneous register;
- distinguish reported facts from independently verified facts;
- do not infer misconduct, institutional complicity or legal liability;
- publish no outcome that was not followed and documented.

### G — Gurjas engagement

Use only for work undertaken after Gurjas Evidence and Policy Analytics was established on 29 June 2026.

Required controls:

- signed or otherwise documented scope;
- engagement dates;
- Gurjas role and deliverables;
- observable outcome rather than causal or promotional language;
- permission status for any institution, person, quotation or identifying detail;
- evidence retained outside the public repository;
- confidentiality and re-identification review.

### N — Not publishable

Do not publish:

- client, scholar, supervisor, employee, consultant or institutional identity without written authority;
- private correspondence, invoices, datasets, theses or screenshots containing identifying information;
- unsupported allegations of fraud, fabrication, corruption, predation or institutional involvement;
- confidential details that can be combined to re-identify a person;
- invented client counts, success rates, payment totals or outcomes;
- claims that Gurjas delivered work performed before the practice existed;
- claims of thesis authorship, guaranteed publication, guaranteed degree completion or guaranteed institutional acceptance.

## Public record schema

Each individual case note must contain:

1. **Record ID** — non-identifying public reference.
2. **Attribution class** — V, F or G.
3. **Period** — exact where public; broad where confidentiality requires.
4. **Context** — client or project type at the least identifying useful level.
5. **Initial problem** — observable or reported, labelled accordingly.
6. **Role** — what Dr. Singh or Gurjas actually did.
7. **Methods** — only methods actually used.
8. **Deliverables** — only files or outputs actually produced.
9. **Outcome** — documented observable outcome; omit when unknown.
10. **Evidence basis** — public source or private evidence category.
11. **Limitations** — what the record does not establish.
12. **Disclosure approval** — owner approval plus client/institutional approval where identification or quotation is involved.
13. **Last reviewed** — date and reviewer.

## Pattern-note rules

A recurring pattern note may describe common sequences and safeguards but must not be written as though one composite person experienced every feature.

Acceptable:

> Across multiple confidential enquiries, researchers described missed deadlines, opaque working files and analysis that did not align with their stated research questions.

Not acceptable:

> A client paid ₹1.5 lakh, then ₹50,000, received fabricated data and was rescued by Gurjas.

The second version implies a verified individual case, a finding of fabrication, a Gurjas organisational role and a documented rescue outcome. It may be used only if each element is evidenced and disclosure-approved.

## Payment and prevalence claims

Amounts may be published only as:

- a documented individual amount with permission; or
- an explicitly labelled reported range in a founder-attested pattern.

Words such as “hundreds”, “daily”, “most”, “commonly” or “widespread” require a defined evidence basis. Without a register, use restrained wording such as “recurring enquiries” or “multiple confidential reports”.

## Journal-identity cases

Named journal or domain cases require:

- first-party publisher or association statement where available;
- exact title and ISSN/ISSN-L comparison;
- publisher and official-domain comparison;
- current database record and coverage dates;
- archived or dated screenshots;
- neutral language separating identity mismatch from legal findings;
- no allegation against an intermediary, institution or individual without authoritative evidence.

Search-engine discovery is never evidence of Scopus, Web of Science, ABDC, UGC or institutional recognition.

## Thesis and manuscript language

Preferred terms:

- research-design advisory;
- methodology review;
- data-provenance assessment;
- statistical analysis and reproducibility support;
- results interpretation;
- chapter review and revision guidance;
- journal identity and fit review;
- manuscript-readiness review;
- reviewer-response support.

Prohibited or high-risk terms:

- completed the thesis;
- wrote the dissertation;
- guaranteed publication;
- guaranteed viva or degree;
- secured Q1/Q2 publication;
- fixed or manufactured the data;
- got the paper accepted.

## Evidence storage

No confidential case register, client evidence or identifying files may be committed to this public repository.

The private register must store at minimum:

- internal engagement ID;
- dates;
- attribution class;
- client/project type;
- role and scope;
- methods and deliverables;
- outcome status;
- evidence retained;
- permission and confidentiality status;
- proposed public wording;
- review record.

## Review gate

Before publication, the reviewer must answer yes to all applicable questions:

- Is pre-Gurjas work attributed to the named individual rather than the practice?
- Is every number supported or clearly labelled as reported?
- Does the wording distinguish observation, report, inference and verified finding?
- Could the description re-identify a confidential person or institution?
- Is the outcome observable and documented?
- Does the record avoid implying ghostwriting or academic impersonation?
- Does a named dispute rely on primary evidence and neutral wording?
- Are limitations visible beside the claim rather than buried in a general disclaimer?

Any “no” blocks publication.
