# South Asia Evidence & Inclusion Lab — founding pilot

> **DRAFT CONCEPT — NOT AN AMDISA, SOUTH ASIAN UNIVERSITY, SAEIC OR SAARC PROGRAMME.**  
> No organisation is a partner, sponsor or endorser unless a later written agreement states the exact role. No logo may be added without written permission.

**Version:** 0.1  
**Prepared:** 31 July 2026  
**Proposed duration:** 90 days  
**Geographic design:** India plus one participating institution or expert focal point from at least three other South Asian countries

## 1. Decision problem

Universities, research cells and civil-society organisations often possess large volumes of reports, datasets and compliance records but lack a common method for testing whether those materials are traceable, decision-relevant and safe to use in institutional or policy claims. Cross-country learning is further weakened when each participant uses different definitions, evidence labels and review procedures.

The pilot will test whether a small regional group can use one transparent evidence rubric, compare recurring gaps without ranking institutions, and produce practical open materials that remain useful after the pilot.

## 2. Proposed outcome

By day 90, participating focal points should have:

1. applied a common 25-item evidence-quality rubric to a bounded sample of public or authorised materials;
2. documented their methods, limitations and unresolved gaps;
3. completed one cross-country methods clinic;
4. contributed only approved, non-sensitive material to a public repository; and
5. reviewed a concise comparative brief that distinguishes common patterns from country- or institution-specific observations.

This is a capacity-building and methods pilot. It is not an accreditation, audit opinion, institutional ranking, certification or impact evaluation.

## 3. Outputs

- A versioned 25-item evidence and inclusion rubric.
- A data dictionary and source-to-claim traceability template.
- One 90-minute virtual methods clinic with exercises and feedback record.
- A controlled public repository containing the rubric, blank templates, method note and approved illustrative examples.
- A maximum 12-page comparative brief covering method, observed patterns, limitations, participant acknowledgements and next-stage options.
- A one-page completion record for each participating organisation or focal point, confirmed factually by that participant.
- A next-stage proposal only if participants confirm that further work is useful.

## 4. Explicit exclusions

The pilot will not:

- use the SAARC emblem or claim SAARC recognition;
- use AMDISA, SAU, SAEIC or participant logos without written permission;
- identify an organisation as a partner before its authorised representative approves the wording;
- collect sensitive personal data, unpublished student records or confidential client data;
- rank countries, institutions, journals, researchers or programmes;
- issue accreditation, compliance, legitimacy or quality verdicts;
- guarantee funding, publication, policy adoption or institutional outcomes;
- offer paid authorship, guaranteed co-authorship or publication preference;
- publish participant-supplied material without a recorded permission decision.

## 5. Participation model

Each participating organisation or focal point would be asked to provide:

- one named methods focal person;
- up to ten public or explicitly authorised evidence examples;
- one short presentation during the methods clinic;
- factual review of references to its contribution;
- a permission decision for any acknowledgement or illustrative example;
- disclosure of relevant institutional, financial or authorship conflicts.

Participation does not require public endorsement of Gurjas. A participant may contribute privately, request anonymisation or withdraw unpublished material before the repository release.

## 6. Responsibilities

### Gurjas

- maintain the rubric, templates, version history and decision log;
- facilitate the clinic and document attendance and feedback;
- apply the same method to all material;
- protect confidential or restricted material from publication;
- draft the comparative brief and provide a factual-review window;
- publish limitations and disagreements rather than forcing consensus;
- retain a clear distinction between Gurjas analysis and participant statements.

### Participating focal points

- confirm that submitted material may be used for the agreed purpose;
- avoid submitting personal or confidential data unless a separate secure agreement exists;
- review descriptions of their own contribution for factual accuracy;
- meet agreed deadlines or notify the group when withdrawal is necessary;
- avoid presenting the pilot as an official inter-governmental or association programme.

### Convening or supporting organisation, if any

A later written note must define whether an organisation is merely circulating an invitation, hosting a clinic, providing technical input, funding work or formally partnering. These roles are not interchangeable and must not be collapsed into the word “partner.”

## 7. Proposed 90-day sequence

| Period | Activity | Evidence of completion |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–10 | Confirm scope, focal points, permitted material and conflicts | Participation records and permission matrix |
| Days 11–25 | Finalise rubric, orientation and sample selection | Rubric v0.9, data dictionary and decision log |
| Days 26–50 | Participant application and office-hour support | Completed local working files and issue register |
| Days 51–60 | Cross-country methods clinic | Agenda, attendance, exercises and feedback summary |
| Days 61–75 | Comparative synthesis and repository preparation | Draft brief, repository manifest and redaction check |
| Days 76–85 | Factual review and disagreement resolution | Comment log and approved acknowledgements |
| Days 86–90 | Controlled release and next-stage decision | Published approved outputs and close-out record |

## 8. Success measures and stop rules

### Minimum success measures

- four country perspectives represented, including India;
- at least three documented institutional relationships or authorised focal-point contributions;
- 80% or more of invited participants complete the rubric or formally close their participation;
- all public examples have an explicit permission record;
- no serious privacy, authorship or affiliation incident;
- at least one participant requests a defined next-stage assignment, clinic or proposal.

### Stop or redesign the pilot when

- fewer than three non-India country focal points confirm by the recruitment deadline;
- requested data cannot be handled safely within the agreed infrastructure;
- a convening organisation requires unsupported endorsement language or logo use;
- participants expect rankings, certification, paid authorship or guaranteed publication;
- the workload cannot be completed without compromising method or review quality.

## 9. Resourcing and budget route

The concept can begin with a no-fee scoping conversation or a single exploratory clinic. A full 90-day pilot requires an agreed scope, staff effort, data-handling plan, hosting responsibilities, payment terms where applicable and a contingency for accessibility, translation or country-specific support. No budget or fee is approved by this draft.

## 10. Approval checklist before external use

- [ ] All named organisations are removed or have approved their exact role wording.
- [ ] Current contact, programme and fee facts are rechecked from primary sources.

- [ ] Budget and payment terms, if any, are separately approved.
