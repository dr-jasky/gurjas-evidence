Recurring pattern 01 · Founder-attested

When an outsourced thesis shortcut becomes the longest route

Across multiple confidential enquiries, doctoral researchers described approaching Dr. Singh after earlier providers missed deadlines, supplied incomplete or opaque working files, or delivered analysis that did not match the research questions or doctoral standard expected.

Some researchers reported paying substantial fees—occasionally more than ₹1 lakh—then incurring extension or late-submission costs and paying again for attempted repairs. These accounts were not collected as a formal dataset, and Gurjas does not publish a count of affected researchers or treat the reports as adjudicated findings.

Evidence status

Confidential recurring professional experience. Identities, institutions and providers are withheld. Payment amounts and events are reported by the researchers and were not independently audited.

Responsible recovery

What must be established before analysis can be defended

  • Research questions, objectives, hypotheses and conceptual framework are aligned
  • The questionnaire or source-data structure measures the intended constructs
  • Sampling, collection and data provenance can be documented
  • Coding, missingness, response patterns and transformations are inspectable
  • The chosen analysis follows from the design rather than software availability
  • Tables, figures and interpretations can be reproduced from retained files
Non-negotiable boundary

Some work cannot be “fixed” through editing

Where the instrument is invalid, the data cannot be verified or the evidence cannot support the stated claims, the responsible recommendation may be a pilot, genuine recollection, redesign or narrower research question.

Gurjas does not manufacture responses, disguise data problems, write a thesis for submission under another person’s authorship or guarantee supervisor, viva or degree outcomes.

Recurring pattern 02 · Founder-attested

A familiar journal title is not the same as a verified journal identity

Researchers and faculty members have repeatedly sought advice after paying publication intermediaries for routes represented as ABDC-ranked or Scopus-indexed. Some described payments of approximately ₹50,000–₹60,000.

In these enquiries, an article appearing on a professional-looking website or becoming discoverable through Google did not establish that it belonged to the exact serial carrying the claimed ranking or database coverage. The reports are confidential and were not retained as a formal incident register, so no numerical estimate is published here.

Verification principle

Rankings and database coverage attach to a defined source identity: exact title, ISSN, publisher, official domain and coverage period—not to a title string or search-engine result alone.

  1. Verify the source

    Resolve the journal title, ISSN/ISSN-L, publisher, official domain, submission system and current database record before paying or submitting.

  2. Inspect the research

    Review the original thesis or project, dataset, methods and contribution before deciding whether a publishable paper exists.

  3. Assess journal fit

    Compare aims, recent articles, contribution threshold, methods, reporting requirements and author guidance.

  4. Prepare without guarantees

    Develop the manuscript for the genuine target journal and support revisions, while leaving editorial and peer-review decisions independent.

How experience becomes practice

The safeguards now built into Gurjas

What Gurjas requires

  • Original research materials and a clear account of data provenance
  • A defined research problem before methods or journals are selected
  • Methods that match the design, evidence and intended claims
  • Official-source verification before relying on a journal or metric claim
  • Author understanding, accountability and control of the final work

What Gurjas refuses

  • Fabricated, purchased or knowingly misrepresented datasets
  • Ghostwritten theses or concealed authorship
  • Guaranteed publication, ranking or institutional acceptance
  • Unofficial payment routes presented as journal access
  • Claims that cannot be supported by retained evidence
