UGC Suggestive Parameters: eight criteria and 35 final checks explained

The final UGC framework for choosing peer-reviewed journals contains eight broad criteria and 35 numbered sub-parameters. An earlier draft contained 36. This guide reconciles the two official documents and shows how to use the final framework without turning it into another simplistic approval list.

By Dr. Jaskirat Singh · Published 12 July 2026 · Updated 31 July 2026 · 10 min read

How many UGC Suggestive Parameters are there?

The final 16 July 2025 Annexure contains eight broad criteria and 35 numbered sub-parameters. The earlier 11 February 2025 draft contained 36. The final document—not the draft—is the source to cite in institutional policy, committee records or formal journal assessment.

Read the final UGC notice and Annexure dated 16 July 2025. The 11 February 2025 notice is useful for understanding the consultation draft and discontinuation history.

When the UGC retired the CARE list, it moved from a central lookup towards an evidence-based assessment method. The Suggestive Parameters for choosing peer-reviewed journals describe what researchers and higher education institutions should examine in a journal. They do not name approved titles, issue a score or remove the need to verify the applicable institutional rule.

Where the final framework came from

The UGC's decision to discontinue the CARE list was taken at its 584th meeting on 3 October 2024. The discontinuation notice dated 11 February 2025 included a draft set of parameters for stakeholder feedback. After considering that feedback, the Commission approved the final parameters at its 591st meeting on 24 June 2025 and issued them through a public notice dated 16 July 2025.

The July notice asks higher education institutions and faculty members to adhere to the parameters and suggests that institutions may establish internal mechanisms to evaluate journals. Because the framework is called “suggestive,” it should guide documented judgement rather than be converted into an unsupported automatic pass/fail score.

The eight official criteria

The final Annexure organises 35 numbered checks under the following eight headings. The descriptions below are concise summaries; consult the official Annexure for the controlling wording.

01 · 9 checks

Journal Preliminary Criteria

Identity, ISSN, title, aims and scope, ownership, publisher information, access model, author guidance and transparency of publication charges.

02 · 3 checks

Editorial Board Criteria

Board composition, subject expertise and identifiable institutional affiliations.

03 · 5 checks

Journal Editorial Policy

Peer-review method, review timelines, plagiarism controls, copyright and licensing terms, and editorial transparency.

04 · 2 checks

Journal Quality Criteria—Content

Originality and relevance of published work, together with the scholarly quality and presentation of articles.

05 · 7 checks

Journal Standards

Regularity, timeliness, DOI practice, archiving, website quality and accessibility, including multilingual availability where applicable.

06 · 4 checks

Research Ethics

Ethics policies, conflicts of interest, corrections and retractions, and safeguards against unethical publication practice.

07 · 2 checks

Journal Visibility

Discoverability through recognised indexing or abstracting services and accessibility to the scholarly community.

08 · 3 checks

Journal Impact Criteria

Evidence of scholarly influence, citation-based indicators where relevant and disciplinary standing—without treating any single metric as certification.

Why some sources say 36

The discrepancy comes from comparing the official draft with the official final document. The February draft numbered 36 items and included a separate item called Online Availability under Journal Standards. In the final July Annexure, that separate item is absent and the remaining Journal Standards entries are renumbered, producing 35 final sub-parameters.

This is not merely a difference in how commentators count bullets. It is a draft-versus-final change visible in the two UGC notices. Secondary pages that state 36 may be repeating the consultation draft. For formal use, cite the final July notice and record that the applied framework contains 35 numbered checks.

How to apply the parameters

Use the framework as an evidence register, not a memory test or a total score. Start with facts that can be independently checked: exact title, ISSN/eISSN, publisher, ownership, DOI history, issue regularity and current indexing. Save the primary-source record and date of verification. Then assess the editorial board, peer-review description, ethics policy, charges, recent content and correction practices.

A single critical failure can matter more than several superficial positives. A journal should not clear scrutiny merely because its website displays many policy pages. Conversely, the parameters do not state that every journal must appear in every commercial index. The judgement should distinguish mandatory identity and integrity controls from context-dependent visibility and impact evidence.

Our journal-verification guide gives a source-first sequence, and the free Journal Evidence Checker helps inspect several external records. Neither substitutes for the institution's current policy or a documented human decision.

What institutions should document

An institutional process should identify who performs the assessment, which primary sources are accepted, how ambiguous or conflicting evidence is escalated, how often records are refreshed and who authorises the final decision. The outcome should record the journal, identifiers, evidence date, applicable programme or policy, supporting sources, unresolved limitations and decision owner.

A blank checklist without provenance is weak governance. A stronger system preserves the evidence behind every conclusion and makes clear that historical CARE inclusion, database indexing or a quartile is one signal—not a guarantee of ethical conduct, suitability or future acceptance.

How I use the framework in practice

I use the criteria as a sequence of controls rather than an additive score. Identity and provenance come first because they can expose a cloned or misrepresented title quickly. Current coverage, DOI history, publication regularity and editorial transparency follow. Only after those controls hold do I assess scope fit, content quality, visibility and impact. This order prevents attractive metrics or website claims from overriding a basic integrity failure.

Frequently asked questions

How many checks are in the final UGC Suggestive Parameters?

The final Annexure dated 16 July 2025 contains 35 numbered sub-parameters across eight broad criteria.

Why do some sources say there are 36?

The February 2025 consultation draft contained 36 numbered items. The final July Annexure removed the draft's separate Online Availability item and renumbered the remaining Journal Standards entries, leaving 35.

Do the Suggestive Parameters replace the UGC-CARE list?

They replace the central list-based approach with an assessment framework. The UGC discontinued the CARE list in February 2025 and approved the final parameters at its 591st meeting on 24 June 2025 before issuing them on 16 July 2025.

Are the parameters mandatory?

The UGC calls them suggestive parameters and requests higher education institutions and faculty members to adhere to them. Institutions may establish their own internal mechanisms and additional current requirements, so the applicable written policy should be confirmed.

Which document should be cited?

Cite the final UGC public notice and Annexure dated 16 July 2025. Use the February notice only when discussing the draft or the history of discontinuation.

The draft contained 36 because it separately included Online Availability under Journal Standards. For decisions affecting degrees, appointments, promotions or institutional evaluation, also obtain the current written policy of the relevant institution.
