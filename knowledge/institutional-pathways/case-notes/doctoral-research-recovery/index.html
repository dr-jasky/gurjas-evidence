Recovering a delayed doctoral research project

A recurring institutional problem is mistaken for an analysis problem: the project has tables and chapters, but the research question, instrument, data provenance and analytical claims no longer form one inspectable chain.

Privacy and evidence boundary

This is a composite of recurring process patterns, not a description of one identifiable scholar or institution. It contains no names, dates, correspondence, client documents, private datasets or supervisor allegations. It does not claim a guaranteed completion, publication or degree outcome.

The decision at stake

The practical choice was whether to keep repairing inherited chapters and output tables or stop, preserve what could be verified and rebuild the project from the research question onward. Continuing to patch the visible outputs would have been faster initially, but it would leave the underlying evidence chain unauditable.

What the diagnostic found

  1. Research questions and variables had drifted apart. Several reported constructs were not traceable to the approved objectives.
  2. The instrument history was incomplete. Item sources, pilot decisions, coding rules and validation evidence were not preserved in one record.
  3. Dataset provenance was uncertain. The available file could not fully establish collection dates, exclusions, transformations or the relationship between raw and analysed records.
  4. Analysis had become output-led. Techniques were selected because they appeared in earlier drafts rather than because they answered a defined estimand or decision.

The governed recovery sequence

  1. Freeze the inherited materials. Keep an immutable copy and label every item by source, date and verification status.
  2. Reconstruct the question-to-evidence map. Link each objective to constructs, variables, source items, sampling assumptions and intended analysis.
  3. Separate usable evidence from unsupported material. Retain only records whose provenance and meaning can be explained.
  4. Rebuild before re-analysing. Where the instrument or data cannot support the claims, redesign, pilot and recollect rather than manufacture certainty.
  5. Document every deviation. Preserve what changed, why it changed and which conclusions are no longer available.

What changed

The immediate product was not a promise of acceptance. It was a bounded recovery record: a verified inventory, an objective–variable–analysis map, explicit stop conditions and a defensible next-stage plan. That changed the project from a collection of disconnected outputs into a sequence that a supervisor, ethics body or examiner could inspect.

What this case note cannot establish

The same sequence will not resolve every delayed project. Feasibility depends on approvals, access to source records, time, participant availability and institutional rules. When provenance cannot be restored, the responsible result may be a narrower study, new data collection or a formal change of design.
